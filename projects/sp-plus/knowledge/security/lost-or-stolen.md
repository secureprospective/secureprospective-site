# If this computer is lost or stolen

**Treat a missing computer as a possible client-information incident.** Protect people and accounts first. Do not search for an unverified feature.

## Put physical safety first

If theft is possible, do not confront anyone or return to an unsafe place alone. Use the appropriate emergency or police process. Record the time, last location, asset tag, and whether it was signed in.

On another trusted computer or phone, notify your practice's security, privacy, or incident contact promptly. Follow its incident plan. Do not include passwords, recovery secrets, or client records unless the approved process specifically requires them.

## Protect accounts from another device

List accounts signed in on the computer: email, File Portal, browser or social accounts, meetings, Bitwarden, and AI. On a separate trusted device, use each provider's security page to review sessions, change passwords, and revoke available access.

A password change does not always end every session. Review the device list and follow your practice's procedure. Protect the multi-factor phone, authenticator, backup codes, and recovery email too.

Do not delay because the computer may have encrypted storage. It might be open, powered on, or signed in to a service.

## What SP+ can and cannot do here

No verified SP+ feature can remotely locate, lock, or wipe a missing computer. The local SP+ helper is not remote management. Do not look for a remote-wipe button or install a tracking tool after the fact.

The checked SP+ virtual machine has LUKS2 encryption for the area holding normal work files. It helps only while powered off and locked. Real installation, hardware, and account circumstances were not tested, so it is not proof that a particular computer or every copy of its data is protected.

## If the computer is found

Tell the same incident contact it was recovered. Do not make its first job client work or assume recovery closes the incident. Record where it was found, whether it appeared powered on, and unexpected changes. Follow the practice's decision about inspection, account recovery, and return to work.

Do not enter a recovery secret, remove encryption, or run a repair suggested by an unexpected caller or webpage.

## Keep evidence without spreading it

Keep when it was last seen, who was notified, reviewed accounts, and provider reports in your practice's approved incident record, not public chat or Fin.

A screen photo can expose client material or a secret. Record non-sensitive error wording instead. If required, frame only needed technical detail.

## Prepare before it happens

Know who receives an incident report, keep an approved backup arrangement, and record the computer's identifying details separately. Lock the screen when you step away, but remember that a screen lock does not replace account controls or a response plan.
