# USB drives and external storage

A USB stick, external hard drive, or portable SSD is separate storage. Treat it as a place to copy files deliberately, not as an extension of your computer that is always private, encrypted, or backed up.

## What should happen when you plug one in

SP+ includes the services Dolphin needs for removable storage and common drive formats. Normally, a recognized drive appears in **Dolphin** or through a device notice. Open it there to see its name and contents.

If a drive does not appear, do not install a driver or download a repair tool. First check that the cable is firmly connected and, for an external hard drive, that it has power. Then ask Fin: **"Fin, I plugged in an external drive and cannot see it. Check what this computer detects."**

## Copy files deliberately

Before copying client work to a drive, ask three questions:

1. **Why does this copy need to leave the computer?** A temporary transfer, a handoff, and a backup are different jobs.
2. **Who will have the drive?** A USB stick can be misplaced or plugged into another computer.
3. **Where is the original?** Keep a known local copy until you have confirmed the copy on the drive opens correctly.

Use Dolphin to copy the file, wait for the copy to finish, then open the copied file from the drive once. A file that has a familiar name is not proof that it copied completely.

## Remove the drive safely

When you are done, use the safe-removal control Dolphin shows for the drive. Wait until it says the drive can be removed, then unplug it. Do not pull a drive out while a copy is in progress or while an app is still saving to it. That can leave a file incomplete or damage the drive's file list.

If you cannot tell whether a copy finished or where the removal control is, ask Fin before unplugging: **"Fin, help me safely remove this USB drive. Do not change or delete any files."**

## Do not assume the drive has this computer's protections

The storage inside this computer is encrypted when it is powered off. A USB drive is its own device. Do not assume it has the same protection, password, backup, or access rules.

Do not keep the only copy of important work on a USB drive. Do not leave one in a laptop bag, car, printer, or conference room. If you need an encrypted external drive or a practice-approved way to transfer client material, ask Fin or support before using the drive for client files.

## When a drive asks you to do something unexpected

Stop if the computer asks to format, erase, initialize, or repair a drive you expected to contain files. Those choices can be destructive. Ask Fin what it sees and what each choice would do before approving anything.

Useful prompt: **"Fin, this external drive is asking to be formatted or repaired. I expected files on it. Explain what you see before changing anything."**
