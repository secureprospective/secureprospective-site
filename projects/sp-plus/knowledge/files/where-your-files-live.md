# Where your files live

Your everyday files live in your personal home area. You do not need to know its technical name or location. Open **Dolphin**, the Files app, and use the folders you can see there. When this computer is off, that area is on encrypted storage.

## The three folders to recognize

| Folder | Use it for | Do not assume |
|---|---|---|
| **Documents** | Work you want to keep and find again | Saving here does not automatically share or back up a file |
| **Downloads** | Files you just received from a website or email | A download is not organized, reviewed, or ready to send onward |
| **Documents/Fin** | Files Fin creates for you | It is still your job to check the result before using or sharing it |

**Documents** is the sensible local starting point for a letter, spreadsheet, PDF, or working copy. **Downloads** is a landing area. Move a file out of Downloads once you know where it belongs, then check that the copy you need opens before deleting anything.

## What Fin does with files it creates

Fin saves documents it creates in **Documents/Fin**. That keeps its work visible in the same place as your other documents instead of hidden inside an app. Open Dolphin and look in Documents when Fin tells you it made a file.

Fin can help you create or find a file, but it should not make a filing decision about client material without you. Use a clear request such as:

- **"Fin, create a draft for me and save it in Documents/Fin."**
- **"Fin, help me find the file about [description]. Do not move, rename, or delete anything."**
- **"Fin, I need to file this client document. Explain whether it belongs in Private or Shared before we move it."**

Do not paste client information, passwords, recovery information, or a document's contents into a request just to identify a file. Describe it by its purpose instead.

## Local, shared, and online are different choices

Putting a file in Documents keeps a local copy on this computer. It does not decide who else can see it. A shared office folder and the File Portal are separate places with their own access rules. Before moving client work, decide who needs access, then read [Private and Shared: the rule that stops misfiling](private-and-shared.md).

A local copy may be useful even after you share a file elsewhere. It is also another copy to manage. Do not assume that encryption means every copy is backed up, that a shared copy is private, or that deleting one copy deletes the others.

## A simple filing habit

When you save or receive a file:

1. **Name it so you can recognize it later.** Use a clear purpose and date where that helps.
2. **Put the working copy in Documents.** Do not leave important work buried in Downloads.
3. **Decide whether it is Private or Shared before you move it again.** This is a client-data decision, not an app setting.
4. **Open the saved copy once.** Make sure it is the file you expected before closing the original.

If you are unsure, stop before making extra copies or deleting the original. Fin can help you inspect the situation and choose the next safe step.
