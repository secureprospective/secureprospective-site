# PDFs: reading, filling in, signing

PDFs open in **Okular**, the document viewer included with SP+. Double-click a PDF in Files to open it. Treat the original as something to read and preserve, not casually rewrite.

## Read and check a PDF

Use Okular to read a PDF, move between pages, and search for a word or name. Before sending one, check the page count and the pages with signatures, amounts, dates, or client instructions. A normal-looking preview can still omit a final page or attachment.

If you need to mark up a copy for your own review, keep the original unchanged. Use **File**, then **Save As** to make a clearly named working copy first. Reopen the saved copy before sharing it so you know the changes are actually there.

## Fill in a PDF form when it has fields

Some PDFs contain real fillable fields. When Okular detects them, it shows a form notice above the document. Choose **Show Forms**, or use **View** then **Show Forms**, to enter information in those fields.

Not every blank line in a PDF is a form field. If you do not see the form option or cannot click into the expected box, stop rather than forcing text into the page. Ask the sender for a fillable version, or use the approved signing service for that document. Do not upload a client PDF to a free online converter just to make it editable.

After entering information, use **File**, then **Save As** for a new copy. Open that copy again and check that the entries remained before you send it. If it offers an Okular archive instead of a PDF, stop and ask Fin before sending it.

## Know which kind of signature you need

"Sign a PDF" can mean two different things:

- An **e-signature request** usually comes from a service your practice already uses. Open the request from the known sender or official service page, review the document, and complete the signing process there.
- A **digital certificate signature** is a technical certificate attached to the PDF. Okular can inspect an existing PDF signature and includes a **Tools** option to digitally sign a PDF, but it needs a signing certificate already provided to the computer. Okular does not create that certificate for you.

If you are unsure which is required, ask the sender or Fin before changing the file. Follow the signing method the sender requires.

## Keep client documents in the right place

Save a working PDF where the right people can see it. **Private** is for you alone. **Shared** is for files SecureProspective needs to see. Before attaching or uploading it, confirm the file name, recipient, and final version. A second copy is easier to make than to retrieve a wrong one after it leaves your control.

Do not give Fin a client PDF, its contents, a password, or a signing certificate. You can still ask for general guidance: **"Fin, this PDF is supposed to have form fields. Explain how I can check that in Okular without opening or uploading the document."**

## When Okular is not enough

A protected form, e-signature platform, or certificate requirement may need approved software or a service. Do not guess at an installer or website. Ask Fin: **"Fin, I need an approved way to [read, fill, or sign] a PDF. Explain the safe next step before adding or changing anything."**
