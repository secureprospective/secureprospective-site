# Finding your way around

You do not need to memorize the whole desktop. Learn the few places that answer most ordinary questions, then use Fin when a task does not match what you see. SP+ is set up so the useful controls are together rather than scattered through technical screens.

## The panel is your quick map

The desktop panel contains four everyday things:

- **The Applications menu** opens the list of programs, including Fin, Dolphin, your browser, and System Settings.
- **Your open apps** appear there while you work. Choose one to return to its window instead of opening another copy.
- **The small status controls** show everyday computer matters such as connections and sound.
- **The clock** is at the end of the panel.

You do not need to know the names of these parts. If someone tells you to use an unfamiliar item, ask Fin: **"Fin, where is [name of the thing] on my desktop?"**

## Open the right app for the job

Two apps matter more than their names suggest:

| When you want to | Open | Why |
|---|---|---|
| See, organize, or open a file | **Dolphin** | This is the SP+ file manager, like File Explorer in Windows |
| Change a computer setting | **System Settings** | This is where computer-wide choices live |

The browser is for the websites where much of your work happens. A browser setting belongs inside the browser. A setting that affects the computer as a whole belongs in System Settings. If that distinction is unclear, say what you want to change and let Fin point you to the right place.

## Do not turn a small problem into a maze

When something looks different from what you expected, pause before changing several settings. Make one visible change, check whether it solved the problem, then decide what to do next. If the screen, sound, printer, or connection is involved, Fin can inspect the computer instead of making you guess.

Useful prompts include:

- **"Fin, I cannot find the setting for [task]. Show me the right place."**
- **"Fin, this part of my desktop looks different. Tell me what it is before I change anything."**
- **"Fin, [sound, printer, display, or connection] is not working. Check it and explain the next safe step."**

## What belongs in a window and what belongs on the computer

An app's own choices affect that app. For example, a website's account choice or a document's font belongs inside the browser or document window. A computer-wide choice, such as how the screen looks or which device plays sound, belongs in System Settings or with the small status controls.

This distinction saves time. It also prevents a harmless browser task from becoming a hunt through every computer setting.

## When you are finished

Close the window you are done with, or leave it open if you expect to return to it. Return to the panel for the next app. If an app stops responding or you are unsure whether it is safe to close, ask Fin before forcing anything.
