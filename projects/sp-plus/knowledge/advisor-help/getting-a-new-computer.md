# Getting a new computer

A new computer is a separate setup, not an instant replacement. The checked SP+ image has no verified migration wizard, automatic backup, or profile-transfer tool. Keep the old computer until the new one passes a normal work check.

## Keep the old computer unchanged at first

Do not erase, sell, return, or hand down the old computer while it may hold client work, account access, or the only file copy. Keep it powered off and physically secure when not using it.

List your work services, approved file locations, and the device holding multi-factor sign-in. Keep passwords, recovery secrets, and client records out of it. You need a map, not secrets.

## Plan the transfer before copying anything

Decide which files move, who approves it, where the new copy belongs, and what proves it worked. A File Portal copy, USB drive, and backup have different access and retention rules.

Do not assume the new computer has the same encryption, recovery-key, account, or update setup. Real installation and hardware setup were not tested. Ask the setup owner to confirm protection and sign-in before client work begins.

## Move files deliberately

SP+ includes Dolphin, the Files app, and removable-drive support. Use only a practice-approved transfer method. A USB drive is separate storage and is not automatically protected like internal storage.

Copy a small approved group first. On the new computer, open copies from their new location and check they are complete. Keep old originals until that succeeds. Do not clone the desktop, browser profile, or hidden settings from an online guide. That can carry old accounts, settings, and problems forward.

## Sign in directly to work services

On the new computer, sign in to email, portals, meetings, and other work accounts through their normal provider pages. Review its device or session list if your practice uses one. Do not copy a browser profile or password file just to avoid signing in again.

Brave's password saving is disabled. Bitwarden is optional, not assumed installed or signed in. Use the approved password method and enter secrets only into the intended service. Do not give passwords, recovery codes, or client information to Fin.

## Test a normal work day before retiring the old one

Use non-sensitive checks: connect to your network, open a known work site, save and reopen a test document, and check needed printer, display, sound, camera, microphone, or headset. Confirm important files are present and the backup arrangement has an owner.

A successful sign-in does not prove every device, account, file, or backup is ready. Physical hardware and a full transfer were not tested. Keep the old computer until approved checks are complete.

## Retire or reuse the old computer only by policy

When the move is confirmed, follow your practice's retention, disposal, and incident rules. Disk encryption helps when powered off, but does not replace an approved decision about records, backups, accounts, and disposal.

If the old computer is missing, treat it as a possible incident. A new one does not make that problem disappear.
