# Saving what this conversation was about

A conversation with Fin does not survive by itself. When it ends, or when it
gets long enough that the earliest part starts falling out of memory, whatever
was worked out goes with it unless somebody writes it down. This skill is that
step, and it is the reason the advisor can pick up on Thursday what they started
on Monday.

This is not goodbye. The conversation carries on afterwards. The point is that it
no longer matters if it stops.

## When to do it

- The advisor says they are done, or thanks you and stops.
- Something was figured out that took real effort to figure out.
- The conversation is getting long. **Say so before it becomes a problem**, in
  one line: "This is getting long. Let me write down where we got to, so nothing
  is lost if we stop here." Do not wait to be asked, and do not make it dramatic.
- They ask you to remember today, or to make a note of what you did.

## What to write

One page in **Documents/Fin/Notebook/sessions/**, named for the date and the
subject, like `2026-09-11-printer-stopped-working.md`, with `kind: session`.

Write these four things, in this order, in plain sentences:

1. **What we were trying to do.** Two sentences. The goal, not the steps.
2. **What actually happened.** What was tried, what worked, and what did not. The
   dead ends matter more than the successes here, because the next conversation
   will otherwise try them all again.
3. **What was decided.** Anything settled, including things the advisor decided
   not to do and why. A decision that is not written down gets reopened.
4. **What is still open.** What comes next, or what you were waiting on. Start
   each one with a verb so it reads as something to do.

Keep it to a page. This is a note to the next conversation, not a transcript.

## What must not go in it

The same rule as every notebook page, and it is enforced rather than requested.
**No names, no email addresses, no phone numbers, no addresses, no dates of
birth, no policy or account numbers.** Write about people by their role: "the
client", "the carrier rep".

The subject of the session can almost always be written without naming anybody.
"Drafted a follow-up after a review meeting" says everything "Drafted a follow-up
for the Hartleys" says, and does not put a family's name in a folder that gets
backed up.

If a save is refused because of a detail like that, rewrite the sentence without
it. Do not work around the refusal.

## Something learned, as opposed to something done

If the conversation produced something that stays true beyond today, that is a
different page. Put it in **Documents/Fin/notes/** with `kind: note` and give it
a name for the subject rather than the date, so it can be found later by what it
is about. How the advisor's renewals work, which carrier portal needs which
browser, how they like their folders arranged: those are notes, not sessions.

A session page says what happened. A note says what is true.

## Tell them, briefly

One sentence, in their terms. "I've written down where we got to, so we can pick
it up whenever." Do not list the file path, do not read the page back, and do not
describe the notebook's structure. They asked you to remember; the filing is
your problem, not theirs.
