# SP+ Trademark Policy

**SecureProspective**, **SP+**, and the SP+ logo are trademarks of
SecureProspective. This policy is separate from the software licenses, and it is
separate on purpose.

## Why this is a separate document

The code is under the GNU GPL, which guarantees anyone the right to use, study,
modify and redistribute it — including commercially. Nothing in this policy
takes any of that away, and nothing in this policy may be read as adding a
restriction to GPL-licensed code. The GPL forbids that, and a license that tried
it would be void.

What a trademark protects is different from what a copyright license protects.
The license governs the **code**. The trademark governs the **name** — whether a
thing may be *called* SP+ and presented to an advisor as the build we stand
behind. Those are separate rights, and conflating them is how projects lose
control of one while trying to protect the other.

## What you may do without asking

- Use, run, and install SP+ for any purpose, commercial or not.
- Study and modify the source, and distribute your modified version.
- Say truthfully that your work is *based on* or *derived from* SP+.
- Refer to SP+ by name in commentary, comparison, review, or documentation.

That last one is nominative use, and it does not require permission. Saying
"compatible with SP+" or "a fork of SP+" is accurate speech about a real thing.

## What requires written permission

- Distributing a **modified** build under the name SP+ or SecureProspective.
- Using the SP+ logo or wordmark as the identity of your own product or service.
- Naming a product, company, or domain in a way likely to suggest that
  SecureProspective published, endorsed, or supports it.

The reason is narrow and specific: an advisor downloading something called SP+
is relying on where it came from. A modified build under the same name breaks a
trust assumption that the person cannot check for themselves. Rename your fork
and everything else here stays open to you.
